# Creator Economy Trends

Mix of stats, observations, and my own thoughts. Some of this is for the newsletter, some is just me trying to understand the landscape I operate in.

## The Numbers
- Creator economy estimated at $250B+ in 2025 (various sources, numbers vary wildly)
- Newsletter platforms (Substack, Beehiiv, ConvertKit, Ghost) have seen massive growth — Substack alone claims 35M+ active subscriptions
- But the median income for newsletter creators is... not great. Most people making real money are in the top 5%
- The "middle class" of creators is growing but slowly. Most people either make nothing or make a lot. Not much in between.

## Newsletter Landscape
- Substack got all the hype but Beehiiv is growing fast, especially for business/marketing newsletters
- Ghost is interesting — open source, you own your platform. More work but more control.
- ConvertKit (now Kit) still dominant for course creators and coaches
- I'm on ConvertKit for The Synthesis. It works fine. Not exciting but reliable.
- The platform matters less than the content and consistency (she says, while being inconsistent about publishing)

## Trends I'm Seeing

### 1. The Unbundling of Media
Traditional publications are losing writers to independent platforms. Why write for a magazine that pays $1/word when you could build your own audience and keep 100% of revenue? Of course the answer is: reach, credibility, editorial support, and guaranteed payment. But the calculus is shifting.

Relevant to me: I do both. Client work for guaranteed income, newsletter for audience building. It's the barbell strategy.

### 2. Knowledge as Product
More creators selling courses, templates, and "second brain" systems than ever. Some of this is genuinely valuable. Some of it is people selling the pickaxes in a gold rush. The knowledge management space especially feels saturated with "here's my Notion template" products.

### 3. AI as Equalizer (and Threat)
AI tools are lowering the barrier to create content. Good for accessibility, concerning for differentiation. If anyone can produce decent content with AI, what makes YOUR content worth paying for? Answer: voice, perspective, curation, trust.

This is directly relevant to the [[AI Writing Partner - Brief|Horizon piece]].

### 4. Community Over Content
The shift from "I publish, you consume" to "we're in this together." Paid communities, Discord servers, cohort-based courses. The content is the entry point; the community is the product.

I haven't done this with The Synthesis. Should I? A Slack group or Discord for subscribers? Seems like a lot of work for 1,200 people.

### 5. The Sustainability Question
Burnout is rampant. The "publish or perish" pressure that used to be an academic thing is now a creator thing. Algorithms reward consistency, audiences expect regularity, but humans need rest. I feel this tension constantly.

## My Position
- 1,200 subscribers on The Synthesis — growing slowly but organically
- Revenue from newsletter: basically nothing right now (no paid tier yet)
- Most income from freelance client work: Horizon, Bloom, ClearPath
- The newsletter is a long-term play: build audience, establish authority, eventually launch paid tier or products
- Possible products: writing course? Templates? The [[Book idea fragments|book idea]]?

## Questions I'm Sitting With
- When do I launch a paid tier? Need more subscribers first? Or does a paid tier help filter for engaged subscribers?
- Is the "creator economy" just freelancing with better marketing?
- How do I differentiate in a world where AI can produce "good enough" content?
- Should I niche down more? "Creativity and technology" is broad.
