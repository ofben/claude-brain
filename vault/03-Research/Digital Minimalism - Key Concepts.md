# Digital Minimalism — Key Concepts

Mostly from Cal Newport's book but also from blog posts, podcasts, and my own thinking.

## Core Idea
Digital minimalism is a philosophy of technology use where you focus your online time on a small number of carefully selected activities that strongly support things you value, and then happily miss out on everything else.

It's NOT about rejecting technology. It's about being intentional about which technologies you adopt and how you use them.

## Key Principles

### 1. Clutter is costly
Having too many apps, services, and digital commitments creates a cumulative drag on your time and attention that's easy to underestimate. Each individual tool seems harmless but together they fragment your focus.

### 2. Optimization is important
Just adopting a tool isn't enough — you need to think carefully about HOW you use it. Most people use social media, for example, in the default mode (endless scrolling) rather than optimizing for specific value (connecting with close friends, following specific topics).

### 3. Intentionality is satisfying
People who deliberately choose their digital tools report higher satisfaction than those who passively accumulate them. The act of choosing is itself valuable — it puts you in control.

## Practices
- **The Digital Declutter:** 30-day break from optional technologies, then selectively reintroduce only what serves clear values
- **Solitude Deprivation:** Modern problem — we're never alone with our thoughts because there's always a screen. Schedule regular "solitude" time.
- **Leisure Quality:** Replace low-quality digital leisure (scrolling) with high-quality analog leisure (crafts, exercise, face-to-face socializing)
- **Attention Resistance:** Be aware of how apps are designed to capture your attention and actively resist those design patterns
