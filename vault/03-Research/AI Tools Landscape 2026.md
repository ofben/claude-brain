# AI Tools Landscape — 2026

Quick survey of what's out there, focused on tools relevant to writers and thinkers. Not comprehensive, just the ones I've actually tried or heard credible things about.

## General AI Assistants
- **ChatGPT (OpenAI)** — The default. Good for brainstorming, research summaries, first drafts of routine content. Feels like a very eager intern. The voice mode is genuinely useful for thinking out loud. Downside: can be confidently wrong, output has that "AI voice" if you're not careful.
- **Claude (Anthropic)** — My current favorite for longer, more nuanced work. Better at following complex instructions and maintaining consistency. Feels more like a collaborator than a tool. Less likely to hallucinate than ChatGPT in my experience, though neither is perfect.
- **Gemini (Google)** — Integrated with Google Workspace which is convenient. Good for summarizing long documents. Hasn't clicked for me as a writing tool though.

## Writing-Specific Tools
- **Sudowrite** — Designed for fiction writers. Can continue your prose in your style, suggest plot directions, describe scenes. I've heard fiction writers love it but it's not really my use case.
- **Lex** — Writing editor with AI built in. Clean interface, the AI suggestions feel less intrusive than other tools. Worth trying for long-form writing. Haven't used it enough to judge fairly.
- **Jasper** — Marketing/content focused. Feels too templated for my taste — it's optimized for "10 Ways to Boost Your SEO" type content, not the kind of writing I do.
- **Copy.ai** — Similar to Jasper. Fine for social posts and ad copy. Not for feature articles.

## Note-Taking & Thinking Tools
- **Obsidian** — Just started using this. Local files, markdown, powerful linking. The "tools for thought" approach. Not AI-native but has community plugins for AI integration. See [[Zettelkasten Method]] for the philosophy behind it.
- **Notion AI** — AI built into Notion's workspace. Good for summarizing meeting notes, organizing databases. The AI is fine but I'm more interested in the structural/linking capabilities.
- **Mem** — AI-powered note-taking. Supposed to surface connections automatically. Haven't tried it but the concept is interesting — could be less manual than Obsidian's approach?
- **Reflect** — Similar space. Backlinks + AI. Clean design.

## The Bigger Picture
What I'm noticing: the most interesting tools aren't the ones that write FOR you. They're the ones that help you think better, organize better, and find connections you might have missed. The "tools for thought" framing is more compelling to me than the "AI writing assistant" framing.

The tools that excite me most right now:
1. Obsidian (for connecting ideas)
2. Claude (for thinking through complex topics)
3. Something that doesn't exist yet — an AI layer that sits on top of your notes and helps you see patterns
