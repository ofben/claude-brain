# The Zettelkasten Method

I just discovered this and I'm kind of obsessed. Documenting everything I'm learning.

## What Is It?
Zettelkasten is German for "slip box." It's a note-taking and knowledge management method developed by Niklas Luhmann, a German sociologist who was absurdly productive — he published over 70 books and nearly 400 academic papers over his career. When people asked how he did it, he pointed to his Zettelkasten: a collection of around 90,000 index cards, each containing a single idea, all connected by a system of references and links.

The basic claim: the Zettelkasten wasn't just a storage system. It was a *thinking partner*. Luhmann said his slip box surprised him — that he would follow a chain of linked notes and arrive at ideas he hadn't consciously thought of. The connections between notes generated insights that neither he nor any individual note could have produced alone.

## Core Principles

### 1. Atomicity
Each note should contain one idea, one concept, one thought. Not a summary of an entire book — a single insight from that book. This makes notes reusable across different contexts.

### 2. Connectivity
Every note should be linked to at least one other note. The links ARE the system. A note with no links is an orphan — it might as well not exist. You don't organize by category (like folders); you organize by connection (like a web).

### 3. Write in Your Own Words
Don't just copy quotes. Translate ideas into your own language. This forces understanding and makes the notes genuinely yours. The act of rewriting IS the act of thinking.

### 4. Bottom-Up Organization
Don't create a taxonomy in advance and try to sort notes into it. Let the structure emerge from the notes themselves. Topics become apparent as clusters of connected notes form.

### 5. The Permanent Note
There are different types of notes in the Zettelkasten:
- **Fleeting notes** — quick captures, temporary, like my [[00-Inbox/|inbox notes]]
- **Literature notes** — notes from reading, summarizing sources
- **Permanent notes** — the refined, atomic, linked notes that live in the system long-term

The key workflow: fleeting notes → process into permanent notes → link to existing notes → discard the fleeting note.

## Why This Matters for Writers

OK this is the part that really got me excited.

As a writer, I'm constantly:
- Researching topics across multiple projects
- Having ideas that connect to other ideas
- Forgetting insights I had three months ago
- Starting from scratch on every new piece when I KNOW I've thought about related things before

The Zettelkasten approach means that research for one article naturally feeds into other articles. My notes on [[Digital Minimalism - Key Concepts|digital minimalism]] for the Bloom series can connect to my notes on [[AI Tools Landscape 2026|AI tools]] for the Horizon piece — because at some level they're about the same thing: our relationship with technology.

Over time, writing becomes less about starting from zero and more about discovering what you already know, finding the threads between existing ideas, and weaving them into something new.

This is EXACTLY what I've been missing.

## How I'm Implementing This

I'm adapting the method for Obsidian rather than doing the pure index-card thing. My approach:
- Use the [[00-Inbox/|Inbox folder]] as the capture point (fleeting notes)
- Process inbox notes into proper notes in Research or a future permanent notes folder
- Link everything aggressively — when I mention a concept, I link it
- Write notes in my own words, not just copy-paste
- Don't over-organize — let the links do the organizing

I'm not going full orthodox Zettelkasten (numbering systems, strict workflows). I'm taking the principles that resonate and adapting them to how I actually work. Purists would probably hate my approach. I don't care — it needs to serve my writing, not the other way around.
