# Writing Process Checklist

My standard process, refined over six years of freelancing. Not every piece follows every step, but when I skip steps, the quality shows.

---

## 1. Research
- Gather sources — academic papers, articles, interviews, data
- Take notes in my own words (don't just highlight and forget)
- Identify the angle — what's MY take? What hasn't been said?
- Create a research note in the vault if the topic warrants it
- Time box this: research expands to fill available time. Set a limit.

## 2. Outline
- Structure the piece — what's the narrative arc?
- Identify key points and the order they need to appear
- Write the headline and subheadings first
- Note where evidence/quotes will go
- Share with editor/client if required (ClearPath wants outline approval)

## 3. Rough Draft
- Get words on the page. DO NOT EDIT AS YOU GO.
- Write ugly. Write fast. Write the bad version.
- If stuck on a section, leave a bracket note [TODO: finish this] and move on
- Target 120% of final word count — it's easier to cut than to add
- Use AI for brainstorming or getting unstuck, but don't let it write the draft

## 4. Cool Down
- Walk away for AT LEAST a few hours. Overnight is better.
- Do something else — a different project, exercise, errands, anything
- The cool down is where your subconscious does the work you can't do consciously
- **This is the step I always want to skip. Don't skip it. The draft is never as good or as bad as you think it is right after you write it.**

## 5. Review
- Read the whole piece aloud — you'll catch awkward phrasing, rhythm issues, and repetition
- Check flow: does each section lead naturally to the next?
- Tighten language — cut filler words, reduce adverbs, kill your darlings
- Verify facts, stats, and quotes
- Check against client voice guidelines if applicable

## 6. Polish
- Final formatting: subheadings, bullet points, paragraph length
- Add links (internal and external)
- Images or visuals if needed
- SEO optimization if required (ClearPath and Bloom both want this)
- Proofread for typos, grammar, consistency

## 7. Submit
- Follow client-specific submission process
- ClearPath: Google Doc, share with James, leave comments on anything uncertain
- Bloom: Google Doc, share with Maya, include alt text for any images
- Horizon: Word doc attached to email to Sarah, include a brief cover note
- Newsletter: Substack editor, schedule for Monday morning send
- ALWAYS re-read one final time before hitting send

---

## What Usually Goes Wrong

The bottleneck is almost always between steps 3 and 4. I write the rough draft and then immediately start editing because the urge to fix things is overwhelming. But editing a fresh draft is like trying to see clearly in a room you just walked into — your eyes haven't adjusted yet. The cool down is what lets you see the piece as a reader would, not as the writer who just sweated through it.

The other common failure mode: spending too long on step 1 (research) as a way of avoiding step 3 (writing). Research feels productive. It IS productive — up to a point. Past that point, it's procrastination in a lab coat.

Rule of thumb: if I've been researching for twice as long as I expected, I'm avoiding the draft. Stop reading. Start writing.
