# AI Writing Partner — Research Notes

Collecting everything here as I find it. Will organize later. (Famous last words.)

## Stats & Data Points
- 73% of professional writers have tried AI writing tools at least once (AuthorGuild survey, Jan 2026)
- But only 28% use them regularly in their workflow
- Most common use: brainstorming and idea generation (not actual drafting)
- The "productivity paradox" — writers using AI tools report saving time on individual tasks but not producing more overall. Why? Because they spend time editing AI output back to their voice.

## Interesting Quotes I've Found
- "AI is the best intern I've ever had — enthusiastic, tireless, and completely lacking judgment." — Marina Kessler, novelist, in The Atlantic interview
- "The danger isn't that AI will replace writers. It's that writers will start thinking like AI." — someone on Twitter, need to find original source
- "I use ChatGPT the way I used to use a thesaurus — to unstick my thinking, not to do the thinking for me." — from a Reddit thread in r/freelanceWriters

## My Own Observations
- I use AI tools mostly for: research summaries, brainstorming angles, drafting email responses to clients, getting past blank page paralysis
- I DON'T use them for: final prose, anything that needs my specific voice, opinion pieces, personal essays
- The uncanny valley is real — AI prose is technically correct but emotionally flat. You can always tell.
- The best use case I've found: asking AI to argue AGAINST my thesis. It's like having a devil's advocate on demand.

## Case Studies / Examples
- **The Verge piece** (Feb 2026) about AI ghostwriting in content marketing — some agencies are now fully AI-written with human "editors." Clients can't always tell. Ethical implications.
- **Wired story** about poets using AI as creative collaborators, not replacers. Interesting distinction between AI as tool vs AI as co-author.
- **GitLab's documentation team** uses AI for first drafts of technical docs, humans refine. They say it's cut production time by 40% without quality loss. But technical docs ≠ creative writing.

## Themes Emerging
1. The "voice" problem — AI can mimic but not originate a writing voice
2. The productivity trap — saving time on individual tasks doesn't equal more/better output
3. The disclosure question — when do you tell editors/clients you used AI?
4. The craft concern — will over-reliance on AI erode writing skills over time?
5. The access divide — AI tools as advantage for some, threat to others
