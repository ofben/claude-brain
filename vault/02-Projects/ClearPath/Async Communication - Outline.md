# The Case for Async-First Communication (Working Title)

Blog post for ClearPath's blog about why teams should default to asynchronous communication and use synchronous (meetings, calls) intentionally.

## Target keyword
"async first communication" / "asynchronous communication best practices"

## Opening Hook
The stat about meetings — something like "the average knowledge worker spends X hours per week in meetings" — then pivot to the question: what if most of those meetings were emails? Or Loom videos? Or shared docs?

See [[Remote Work Communication Research]] for the stats I'm pulling from.

## Section 1: The Meeting Problem
- Everyone agrees there are too many meetings. The data backs this up.
- But it's not just about quantity — it's about the hidden costs. Context switching, preparation time, the meeting-after-the-meeting.
- Cal Newport's "hyperactive hive mind" concept — our default is to just talk about everything in real-time, and it's killing deep work.

## Section 2: What Async-First Actually Means
- Define it clearly: async-first doesn't mean no meetings. It means meetings are the EXCEPTION, not the default.
- Default communication through written channels (docs, messages, project tools)
- Meetings reserved for: complex discussions, relationship building, sensitive topics, brainstorming
- **NEED TO FLESH THIS OUT — maybe a simple framework or decision tree?**

## Section 3: Benefits
- Deep work becomes possible — no more fragmented days
- Better documentation — async communication creates a paper trail
- Inclusivity — works across time zones, accommodates different working styles
- The "triple peak day" finding from Microsoft — people naturally work in bursts, async supports this
- Better decisions — written communication forces clearer thinking

## Section 4: How to Make the Shift
- Start with a communication audit
- Create channel guidelines (what goes where)
- Set response time expectations (not everything needs an answer in 5 minutes)
- Use async video (Loom, etc.) for anything that needs tone or nuance
- Protect "maker time" on calendars
- **This section needs more specific, practical advice. Check ClearPath's product features — can I reference their tool naturally here?**

## Section 5: When Sync is Still Better
- Don't strawman meetings — they have real value
- 1:1s for relationship building
- Complex negotiations or sensitive conversations
- Team bonding and culture building
- Crisis response
- The key is CHOOSING sync intentionally, not defaulting to it
