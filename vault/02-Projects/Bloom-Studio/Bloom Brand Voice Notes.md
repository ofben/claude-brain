# Bloom Studio — Brand Voice & Guidelines

From onboarding call with Maya (January 2026) and their brand doc.

## Voice
- **Warm but not fluffy** — like a knowledgeable friend, not a guru
- Evidence-based — reference studies and data but explain them in plain language
- Encouraging without being patronizing ("you can do this" not "it's so easy!")
- First person plural sometimes ("we all struggle with...") to build community feel
- Conversational but polished — contractions are fine, slang is not

## Audience
- Women 28-42 (primary), men 28-42 (secondary)
- Professional, often in demanding jobs
- Interested in wellness but skeptical of "woo" — they want practical, grounded advice
- Many are parents or caregivers juggling multiple responsibilities
- Tech-savvy but feeling overwhelmed by technology, not excited by it

## Do's
- Use specific, actionable advice ("try this for one week")
- Include frameworks and mental models people can remember
- Acknowledge that change is hard and progress isn't linear
- Reference research but make it accessible
- Use short paragraphs and subheadings for scannability

## Don'ts
- Don't shame people for their current habits
- Don't use jargon or assume specialized knowledge
- Don't be preachy or absolutist ("you MUST delete social media")
- Don't use gendered language unnecessarily
- Don't promise transformation — promise tools and small improvements
- Avoid: "simply," "just," "easy" (these minimize the reader's struggle)

## Formatting
- Blog posts: 800-1000 words
- Subheadings every 150-200 words
- At least one "try this" actionable takeaway per post
- End with a gentle call to action (newsletter signup or related post)
- SEO: include target keyword in title, first paragraph, and at least 2 subheadings

## Tone Examples
Good: "If your morning starts with 20 minutes of scrolling before your feet hit the floor, you're not alone — and you're not broken. But there might be a better way to start your day."

Bad: "Stop scrolling first thing in the morning! It's ruining your productivity and your brain."

Also bad: "Gently invite yourself to explore the sacred morning space before allowing digital energy into your field."
