# The Synthesis — Newsletter Ideas Backlog

Running list of ideas for future issues. The Synthesis is about creativity and technology — how they intersect, clash, and evolve together.

Current subscribers: ~1,200
Publishing cadence: every other week (ish... I've been inconsistent)

---

- [x] **"The Attention Tax"** — Every new tool promises to save time but demands attention. The hidden cost of adding another app to your workflow. *(Used: Issue #31, Feb 2026)*

- [x] **"Writing in the Age of AI"** — Personal essay about my first year using AI tools as a writer. What changed, what didn't. *(Used: Issue #29, Jan 2026)*

- [ ] **"Tools That Think With You"** — The concept of "tools for thought" and why it matters for creators. My journey discovering Obsidian, Zettelkasten, etc. Personal angle about my own note-taking mess. *(Currently drafting — see [[Issue Draft - Tools That Think With You]])*

- [ ] **"The Messy Middle of Any Creative Project"** — That phase where the excitement has worn off and the finish line isn't in sight. How to navigate it. Drawing from my current experience juggling three client projects.

- [ ] **"Your Process Is Your Product"** — For freelancers and creators: the WAY you work is what makes your output distinctive. Systemizing without losing soul.

- [ ] **"The Myth of the Lone Creator"** — How even "solo" creators rely on invisible networks. Editors, peers, communities, tools. Nobody creates alone.

- [ ] **"Digital Gardens vs. Content Farms"** — The difference between growing ideas slowly and churning out content. What if we treated our online presence like a garden instead of a factory?

- [ ] **"What I Learned from Tracking My Creative Energy for 30 Days"** — Actually do this experiment first, then write about it. When am I most creative? What drains me?

- [ ] **"The Portfolio Career"** — Reflections on having multiple clients and projects. The freedom and the fragmentation. Is this the future of work or just freelancing with a nicer name?

- [ ] **"Slow Creativity in a Fast World"** — Against the pressure to produce constantly. Making space for thinking, wandering, and not-knowing. Connects to digital minimalism.

- [ ] **"When Your Notes Talk Back"** — The experience of linking notes and having old ideas suddenly connect to new ones. The serendipity engine. (Save this for after I've been using Obsidian for a while.)
