## The rabbit hole
OK so I went down a MAJOR rabbit hole today. Started with "tools for thought" as a concept for the Horizon piece and ended up reading about the [[Zettelkasten Method]]. It's this note-taking system invented by a German sociologist (Niklas Luhmann) who published like 70 books using it. The basic idea: small atomic notes that link to each other, building a network of ideas over time.

I've been feeling for months like my notes are a mess — stuff scattered across Google Docs, Apple Notes, random text files, bookmarks I never revisit. The Zettelkasten idea is that your notes should be a CONVERSATION with your past self. Each note connects to others and new ideas emerge from the connections.

This led me to discover [[Tools for Thought History|the whole "tools for thought" movement]] — Vannevar Bush's Memex concept from 1945, Doug Engelbart's "Mother of All Demos," Ted Nelson's hypertext. We've been trying to build tools that augment human thinking for 80 years.

And then I found Obsidian. And Logseq. And the whole "personal knowledge management" world. There are people who have built these incredible interconnected note systems and they swear it's changed how they think and write.

Am I about to become one of those people? Maybe. I downloaded Obsidian.
